# A/B Testing Learning Guide

## Introduction

A/B testing is an experimentation method used to compare two versions of a webpage, advertisement, email, or other marketing asset. By measuring how each version performs, marketers can make evidence-based decisions about improving conversions and user engagement.

## Understanding Conversion Rates

A conversion rate is the percentage of visitors who complete a desired action. For example, if 20 out of 400 visitors sign up for a service, the conversion rate is 5%.

## What Is Statistical Significance?

Statistical significance helps assess whether observed differences between variations provide evidence against a null hypothesis. Results should be interpreted alongside sample size, experiment design, and practical business impact.

## Understanding P-Values

A p-value measures how compatible the observed data are with a specified null hypothesis. A small p-value, such as one below a preselected 0.05 threshold, may provide evidence against that hypothesis. It does not show the probability that the hypothesis is true.

## Good Testing Practices

- Define the primary metric before starting an experiment.
- Collect enough data for a meaningful comparison.
- Avoid repeatedly checking results and stopping early without an appropriate statistical method.
- Consider the size and business importance of the observed effect.
- Document the methodology and conclusions.

## Conclusion

A/B testing can support better marketing decisions when experiments are planned carefully and results are interpreted responsibly. Statistical tools are helpful aids, but they should be used alongside sound experimental design and business judgment.
